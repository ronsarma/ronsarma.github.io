This book was recommended by my friend, Uddeepon. This 150-page reader was a helpful way of taking a moment and reflecting, especially given the times that we are living through right now. 
Hoff playfully distills the Chinese concept of Taoism through the life of Winnie the Pooh and his friends Piglet, Rabbit, Eeyore, Owl and Christopher Robin. He connects ideas like *Tao* (the Way) and *Wei Wu Wei* (do without doing) from the traditional Chinese book Tao Te Ching written by Lao-Tzǔ sometime during the 5th Century. 

It is genuinely surprising how relevant simplicity, non-action, nothingness, clarity, humility and balance is in everyday life. I particularly enjoyed this book because it was comforting to see some overlap between some of the core principles of my (*lahe-lahe*) culture/nature and this book. Some quotes from the book - *"flow like water, reflect like a mirror and respond like an echo"*, Chuang-Tzu says, *"Nothing is something to a Taoist. In fact, T'ai Hsü (Great Nothing) is Tao - The Way of the Universe"*, Claude Debussy says, *"Music is in the space between the notes"*, *"the wise are the Children Who Know"*, *"the Forest of Life is in a desperate condition because there are too many who think think too much and care too little"*. 

Peronal take: While I have a fresh perspective on not trying too hard, I also value going the extra mile to find (and perhaps push) your own limits. Coupling simplicity with passionate hardwork feels like something that works for me (sometimes). Finding the balance is definitely a different challenge by itself.
A solid recommendation for anyone feeling overwhelmed by the current times (Oct 2026). This book is a gift.
